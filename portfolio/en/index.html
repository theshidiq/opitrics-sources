One problem, followed through

These are not unrelated side projects. The same operational problem runs through all three: I hit it at work, formalised it for my thesis, then rebuilt it as a product.

  2. 1The workplace

    Where the problem came from

    Kitchen staff scheduling at the hotel took an observed 4–8 hours each cycle, done by hand for 15–20 people. Fairness was difficult to hold to, and constraint violations were common. Allergen data was transcribed from Japanese product labels by hand, which is slow and error-prone.

  3. 2The thesis

    Formalising it

    I modelled scheduling as a combinatorial optimisation problem and solved it with OR-Tools CP-SAT, then verified the result against a 17-case test suite. This became my BINUS final thesis project.

  4. 3The product

    Making it operational

    I rebuilt the ideas for real hotel operations: a multimodal LLM reads Japanese product labels, allergens are derived automatically when a menu is composed, and the system is deployed rather than demonstrated.

Final thesis project · BINUS University

Shift Schedule Manager (OR-Tools)

537 commits · as of July 2026

Problem
Building the kitchen roster for 15–20 staff took an observed 4–8 hours of manual work each cycle at the hotel where I work. Doing it by hand made fairness hard to guarantee and constraint violations frequent.
Outcome
The solver generates a schedule in under 30 seconds, verified across 17 test cases. That replaces an observed 4–8 hours of manual scheduling at my workplace; the 4–8 hour figure is a measurement of the manual process, not a solver benchmark.
Role
Sole developer — requirements, architecture, implementation, verification and deployment.

Key engineering decision: Guaranteeing a usable schedule over mathematical purity

With 12+ constraint types, real rosters regularly produced conflicting requirements and the solver returned INFEASIBLE — mathematically correct and operationally useless, because the manager still needs a roster that shift. I converted selected hard constraints into soft constraints carrying very high penalty weights. The solver now always returns a feasible schedule, and where something had to give, the penalty weighting makes the trade-off visible and reviewable instead of failing silently. Handing back a near-perfect schedule with one flagged compromise beats handing back nothing.

A three-tier architecture: a React frontend, a Go WebSocket server, and a Python optimisation service. The solver itself is around 4,200 lines implementing more than 12 constraint types — one shift per person per day, calendar rules, staff absences, locked cells and leave caps as hard constraints; consecutive-day limits, backup cover, priority rules and fairness balancing as soft constraints. The UI handles Japanese roster notation (△ early, ○ standard, ◇ late, × off) and Japanese-era dates.

Front Hotel Agent

352 commits · as of September 2026

Problem
Allergen information for hotel food service had to be read off Japanese product labels and entered by hand. It was slow, and transcription mistakes carry real consequences when someone has an allergy.
Outcome
A working scan-to-menu pipeline: photograph a label, a multimodal LLM extracts ingredient and allergen data, it is stored in Supabase, and allergens are derived automatically when a menu item is composed. Deployed on Fly.io. I have not instrumented OCR accuracy yet, so I am not publishing a figure for it.
Role
Sole developer — full stack, from OCR pipeline through API design to deployment.

A pnpm monorepo with a SvelteKit 5 frontend using runes for state and a NestJS 11 API documented with Swagger. Qwen VL-OCR via DashScope handles Japanese label recognition. Menu items derive their allergen set from linked products, and dietary badges (vegetarian, vegan, halal, gluten-free) fall out of the same data. I built it aiming at hospitality-industry product companies — the domain I already know from the inside.

Aokun — JLPT & BJT study platform

286 commits · as of November 2026

Problem
Studying for the BJT and JLPT while working full time, I kept losing the thread: material scattered across books and apps, no sense of what to do next, and nothing that fitted the half-hours I actually had.
Outcome
A working study platform with a live demo anyone can open without signing up. Lessons run as interactive video, passages highlight word by word with the audio, and a written-email exercise is graded by an LLM. Deployed on Cloudflare Pages. I use it for my own N2 and BJT study.
Role
Sole developer — product design, frontend, API, media pipeline and deployment.

React 19 and TypeScript on Vite, with Zustand for state and Supabase for sync so progress follows you across devices or stays on-device as a guest. Lesson videos are composed in code with Remotion and played in-page, with live furigana, tap-to-look-up words and quizzes that wait for an answer. Audio is served from Cloudflare R2. The email-practice exercise posts to a Cloudflare Pages Function that calls the DeepSeek API for grading and tutor feedback. The public demo runs entirely from sessionStorage with sample progress, so visitors never touch a real account and the AI route is not called — the sample exchange is fixed and labelled as such in the UI.
